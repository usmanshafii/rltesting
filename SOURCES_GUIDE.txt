KAL MULTISPECTRAL DETECTION FYP (MD-550 / L550E ENGINE VERSION): WHICH SOURCE EACH COMPONENT COMES FROM
===================================================================================================
Updated 2026-10-11. One entry per component: what it does, where it lives, what it is built from, and how
reliable each input is. Engine and solar sources are saved in docs/sources/ (each document, the pages cited, the
images and the digitised data; the register with URLs, pages and SHA-256 is docs/sources/README.md; every reading is
re-derived by tools/source_measurements.py). The full engine derivation with every number is
docs/engine_signature_physics.md; every derived engine number is saved as data in docs/reference/engine/data/ by
tools/engine_report.py.

HOW TO READ THE TAGS
--------------------
Source tiers (CLAUDE.md, rule 2), about the source:
  S1  authoritative and about the exact item (its manufacturer's datasheet, manual or drawing; a standard; a
      regulator's document; a peer-reviewed measurement of the item)
  S2  authoritative, about a close analogue or general physics (a comparable product's manufacturer manual,
      peer-reviewed papers and theses, textbooks, handbooks, government research reports such as NACA)
  S3  secondary (encyclopedias, vendor notes without conditions, press reports): cross-check or lead only
  S4  weak, not used for values (forums, listings, snippets, AI text, unsourced specs)
Evidence grades (configs/signatures.yaml header), about the value as used for the KAL:
  A observed in KAL imagery   B strongly inferred or published   C+ engineering estimate with physics
  C plausible assumption       D unknown
Other tags used in the configs:
  [spec]  a decision recorded in FYP_Build_Spec_Multispectral_Aerial_Detection.md or FYP_Sky_and_Background.md.
          The spec records decisions, not evidence (user, 2026-10-10): such a value is not "sourced".
  [calib] measured on this renderer (render units).
  physics exact laws and standard correlations (energy, mass and momentum balances, Planck, fin theory).

A value is only as strong as its weakest input: a B range computed with one C factor is C for that factor's share.

3. ENGINE COOLING (free-air cooling, as on the original L550E)
-------------------------------------------------------------
What:     The L550E is cooled by the airflow over its cast cylinder fins and its optional finned heat sinks; no
          fan or shroud is listed by Limbach (datasheets, S1). The model implements exactly that: ram air at the
          flight speed x an installation factor, plus the pusher propeller's inflow, over the barrel fins and the
          heat sinks; no fan, no baffles.
Files:    thermal/engine_heat.py (fin_state, heatsink_state, fin_heat_balance, prop_inflow_ratio),
          target/engine_l550e.py (FIN_PACK, HEAD, HEATSINK)
Sources:  NACA Report 488, Biermann and Pinkel (1934): heat transfer from finned cylinders across a free air
            stream, wind tunnel, 30-150 mph; the L550E's fins lie inside its tested ranges (S2, high relevance).
            h ~ V^0.796, h ~ (fin space)^0.322, reference q from its fig. 9 (digitised,
            docs/sources/naca/fig9_digitised.csv; reproduces the report's exponent and quoted values),
            similitude for diameter and density (eqs. 2-6) .................................................. B
          Fin efficiency tanh(mL)/(mL), straight-fin theory (Incropera; NACA 488 eq. 13 form) ............. physics
          Propeller inflow at the engine: actuator-disc momentum theory, ideal-propeller upper bound ....... physics
          Cooling load to be rejected: AFIT (Ausserer 2016) 10-11 % of fuel energy, with Limbach's SFC ...... B-
          Cross-check: laminar channel-flow model (Incropera tab. 8.1, eq. 8.41) gives 22 % less than NACA 488 at
            the envelope centre (54 vs 69 W/m2K: laminar, no free-stream turbulence; tested within 30 %) ....... physics
          Heat sinks: Limbach's option, fitted (drawing and both photos); fins across the stream at the head
            temperature, h = 0.25-1.0 x the NACA 488 value ......................................... B- geometry / C h
Not sourced: installation factor of the local air speed (0.5-0.9 at the cylinders, 0.7-1.0 at the stubs: fuselage
          wake, C); heat-sink h relative to aligned fins (0.25-1.0: its fins stand across the ram air, C);
          head-to-heat-sink root ratio (C+). The temperatures depend weakly on these (fin efficiency 0.8-0.97).
Result:   barrel fins + heat sinks carry the sourced cooling load inside the Rotax CHT band at the best-cooled
          corner of the flight envelope (load / capacity 0.61 / 0.77 / 0.98 at low / nominal / high power); at its
          centre 1.31 / 1.69 / 2.20 (1.12 / 1.36 / 1.68 with the propeller's inflow), so the head runs at the top of
          its band there, as AFIT notes for pusher installations; without the heat sinks the ratios are 15 % higher.
          Data: docs/reference/engine/data/heat_balance.csv, cooling.csv. Test: tests/test_engine_heat.py.

          configs/m1_electric_like.yaml: thermal/engine_heat.py S7 (electric_like_envelope), the engine's own fins and
          heat sinks carrying an electric drive's loss heat (1 - eta) / eta x shaft power; motor efficiency 0.90-0.96
          from EU 2019/1781 Annex I (IE3/IE4 at 15-37 kW, S1/S2) and Hepperle 2012, NATO STO-MP-AVT-209 paper 9
          (motor 95 %, controller 98 %, S2); docs/sources/electric_drive/, checked by electric_drive_readings() .. C+

          docs/engine_signature_physics.md

            limits; CHT at the spark-plug seat like the L550E's thermocouple) and EASA TCDS E.082 (Limbach L 1700,
            250 C, S1/S2) ......................................................................................... B (analogue)
          EGT bands: same Rotax manual (its own 580 C / 1,000 F inconsistency covered) ...................... B (analogue)
          Air/fuel ratio: stoichiometry (oxygen balance) / equivalence ratio 1.0-1.2 of carburetted small
            two-strokes (Ausserer 2016, AFIT; peer reviewed 2018) ............................................ B-
          BSFC: Limbach chart (S1) and Rotax consumptions with EN 228 fuel density ........................... B
          Exhaust stub wall: Gnielinski (1976), Churchill-Bernstein (1977), radiation, NTU ................... physics
          Fuel LHV and heat of vaporisation: Heywood (1988), app. D (S2) ..................................... B
          Exhaust plume (not rendered): LWIR emissivity from the water-vapour continuum (Roberts et al. 1976) and
            the CO2 band edge: 3-15 % of the stubs' contrast (up to 46 % with a x3 margin), inside their own
            emissivity range ........................................................................... physics / C+
Not sourced: low-power EGT floor 400 C (C+), exhaust pulsation factor 1.2-2.0 (C+; Depcik and Assanis 2002 is the
          check, not obtained), part-to-part fractions head/fin-root/case/flange/tail cone (C/C+; no measurement on
          a comparable engine found), carburettor coupling (C), power-setting fractions and weights (coverage, C);
          the SWIR plume (D: not calculated; Planck alone shows a band-average plume emissivity of 0.02-0.5 % would
          equal the stubs, so it needs a sourced hot-gas band model, e.g. NASA SP-3080 or HITEMP).
Temperature bands: the authentic source is Limbach's L550E operating manual (not public; not searched online by
          the user's rule). Placed in docs/sources/limbach/, it replaces the Rotax bands in thermal/engine_heat.py.
Validation within reach: a thermal camera on any running small air-cooled two-stroke (temperature ratios); a CHT
          thermocouple and EGT probe on an L550E-class engine.

4a. NIGHT RGB GLOW OF HOT PARTS
------------------------------
What:     Hot metal glows: (1 - albedo) x Planck radiance in each colour band, added to the RGB material, from
          370 C (below it < 0.1 x the faintest night sky). Only the exhaust stubs get that hot; it shows over the
          night sky in ~2.6 % of all scenes (docs/reference/engine/data/night_glow.csv).
Files:    render/signatures.py (rgb_self_emission, add_rgb_glow_draw), render/rgb.py, render/scene.py,
          configs/signatures.yaml rgb.hot_emission, tests/test_glow_and_glints.py
Sources:  Planck's law and Kirchhoff (exact); render-unit calibration as SWIR: the renderer's sun disc / the
            direct sun in the same band from the ASTM G173-03 standard spectrum (NREL's table, S1):
            r 125.9, g 133.8, b 115.1 W/m2 (docs/sources/astm/band_integrals.csv) ............................ B
          Temperatures: the RGB pass's own draw from the derived LWIR ranges (component 4).
Not sourced: the colour camera's bands r 0.60-0.70, g 0.50-0.60, b 0.40-0.50 um (C+, an IR-cut colour camera);
          the renderer's sun-disc calibration ([calib]).

4b. LWIR SUN GLINTS
-------------------
What:     The specular reflection of the sun's own 8-14 um radiance by every surface with emissivity < 1:
          (1 - eps) x f_s x E_sun / (pi sigma^2) x a Gaussian lobe around the mirror direction, evaluated in the
          emission-only shader. FLIR's note: glints read hundreds of degrees too hot; here a bare-aluminium glint
          reads +16 K (median scene) to +429 K (99th percentile) too hot at the mirror angle
          (docs/reference/engine/data/sun_glint.csv).
Files:    render/signatures.py (sun_glint_params, glint_radiance), render/lwir.py (_glint), render/sampling.py,
          configs/signatures.yaml lwir.sun_glint, tests/test_glow_and_glints.py
Sources:  E_sun above the atmosphere 1.261 W/m2 in 8-14 um: ASTM E490 standard spectrum (NREL's table, S1) ...... B
          Sun's angular radius 0.2665 deg: IAU 2015 Resolution B3 radius / IAU 2012 B2 au (S1) ................ B
          Transmittance (1 - eps_z)^(1 / sin el): the scene's Idso (1981) clear sky (component 7) ........ B- / C+
          FLIR note 16-0539 p. 6: sun glints read hundreds of degrees too hot (S3, qualitative motivation).
Not sourced: lobe width 0.5-6 deg and specular share 0.3-1.0 (C; validate with LWIR images of bare metal and
          painted panels swept through the sun's mirror angle).
Checks:   tests/test_glow_and_glints.py (physics, flux conservation, calibration against the saved standards);
          tools/check_glow_glint_blender.py (the Blender shaders reproduce glint_radiance to 0.005 % and the
          glow to 0.03 %; docs/reference/engine/data/blender_glow_glint_check.json).

RULES (CLAUDE.md)
-----------------
- Look up authentic sources first; estimate only what has none, with physics, a covering range and a grade.
- Judge each source's tier and its relevance to this context; do not use weak sources.
- Save every authentic source used: the document, the pages cited, the images and the digitised data, under
  docs/sources/<source>/, registered in docs/sources/README.md with URL, date and SHA-256; keep the tool that
  read numbers off it (tools/source_measurements.py) so the reading can be repeated.
- The spec is not evidence; ranges taken from it are not sourced until traced.
