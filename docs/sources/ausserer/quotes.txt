Engines. PhD dissertation AFIT-ENY-DS-16-S-055, Air Force Institute of Technology. Distribution A, approved for
public release. Passages used in this project, verbatim, with the printed page (pdf page in brackets).

p. v (pdf 7): "At peak power, the loss pathways ranked by relative magnitude were: short-circuiting (40%-50%),
sensible exhaust enthalpy (14%-18%), incomplete combustion (11%-17%), brake power (14%-15%), and heat loss from the
cylinder (10%-11%)."
  -> used for: cooling load share (thermal/engine_heat.COOLING_SHARE), the energy check of the EGT bands.

p. v (pdf 7): "thermal losses do not begin to increase substantially until engine displacement decreases below
10 cm3."
  -> used for: carrying the 10-11 % cooling share over to the L550E's 137 cm3 cylinders.

p. 270 (pdf 309): "incomplete combustion more than tripled from 11% of total fuel energy at a delivery ratio of 1.1
(WOT) to 36% of the total fuel energy at a delivery ratio of 0.3 (1/4 throttle)."
  -> used for: the delivery-ratio check range (0.25-1.25).

p. 284 (pdf 323): "Small engines are usually operated rich as a cooling measure and to provide a margin against the
wide swings in equivalence ratio typical in simple carburetors over the operating range of such engines."
p. 285 (pdf 324): "the 3W-55i engine was tuned to an equivalence ratio of 1.1 at 6000 rpm, which resulted in
equivalence ratios varying from 0.8 to 1.2 over the entire operational speed range of the engine."
p. 316 (pdf 355): "Typically, small ICEs are air carbureted in their COTS configuration, operating at equivalence
ratios of 1.1 to 1.2."
  -> used for: the air/fuel ratio band (equivalence ratio 1.0-1.2).

p. 296 (pdf 335): "The exhaust gas temperature (EGT) is representative of the short-circuited charge and the burnt
gas from inside the cylinder."
  -> used for: taking the EGT band as the gas temperature entering the exhaust stubs.

p. 299 (pdf 338): "cooling is primarily dependent on aircraft velocity and operators tend to err on the side of over
cooling."
p. 301 (pdf 340): "For pusher configuration aircraft where the engine is only exposed to the free stream, WOT

p. 301 (pdf 340): "the cooling load increased by about 30%. This corresponded to a change in cylinder head
temperature of 60 C."
  -> used for: free-air cooling at the flight speed, and the reading of the heat balance at high power.

p. 307 (pdf 346): "The largest pathway shift was the reduction in short-circuiting, which fell from upwards of 50% at
WOT to 15% to 20% at approximately half throttle."
p. 311 (pdf 350): "despite increases in the relative magnitudes of incomplete combustion and heat transfer, the
improvement in short-circuiting as the engines were throttled led to a 4%-6% (absolute) improvement in fuel
conversion efficiency between WOT and half throttle."
  -> used for: part-throttle BSFC below the full-throttle chart, the cooling share rising at part throttle
     (low-power CHT bound), heat shifting from the exhaust to the head.
