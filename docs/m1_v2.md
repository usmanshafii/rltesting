The booklet's M1 is an MVP / feasibility pilot. The question here is whether the privileged-teacher design is viable
before the full detector (`models/detector.py`) is trained in M2: does a teacher that also sees the cooled-LWIR and MWIR
cameras find the target better than RGB + uncooled LWIR, and does any of that carry into the deployable student?

## Models (identical seeds, crops and budgets)

| Model | Inputs at inference | Training | Laptop |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lwir_only` | LWIR uncooled | supervised | A |
| `lwir_cooled_only` | LWIR cooled (7.7-9.3 µm zoom) | supervised; only on targets its camera sees | A |
| `mwir_only` | MWIR zoom | supervised; only on targets its camera sees | A |
| `rgb_lwir` | RGB + LWIR uncooled | supervised, random band dropout: the matched baseline | B |
| `teacher` | RGB + LWIR uncooled + LWIR cooled + MWIR | supervised on every sensor subset (all four 40 %, else one of the 14 subsets); privileged, needed only for training | B |
| `student` | RGB + LWIR uncooled | as `rgb_lwir` + distillation from `teacher` | B |
| `student_ctrl` | RGB + LWIR uncooled | as `student`, but distilled from `rgb_lwir`: the control | B |

Each model trains for 3,000 steps of 16 crops; validation runs every 250 steps; the best checkpoint is chosen by
validation FROC. Distillation is response (softened heatmap, boxes where the teacher is confident) plus masked feature
distillation at strides 4 and 8, weighted towards objects the teacher sees much better than the student
(`models/cnn_detector.py`). Since 2026-10-18, targets the student's own frames do not show (contrast below 1) are left
out of both terms; before, the weight peaked on them (`docs/privileged_teacher_case.md`, section 6).

**Claims (frozen in the manifest before training).** Distillation is claimed only if the teacher beats `rgb_lwir` on
validation FROC and AP50. A student gain is credited to the cooled-LWIR and MWIR cameras only if `student` also beats
`student_ctrl` on the test scenes (`student_minus_student_ctrl`, paired scene bootstrap); `student_ctrl - rgb_lwir` is
what distillation alone does without any privileged camera.

## Scenes (`configs/m1.yaml`)

600 open-sky scenes, split 60/10/30 (360 training, 60 validation, 180 test), day and night, the six target-size bins
(2-4 to 64+ px) balanced, no hard negatives. Everything else is the project's current scene model:
- Lahore conditions: each scene takes a real station hour (air temperature, humidity, haze/smoke visibility, cloud,
  pressure; `docs/lahore_climate.md`) and the layered thermal atmosphere (`docs/thermal_bands.md`). The six scenes
  rendered so far all have haze at 4-5 km visibility, as 93 % of Lahore's hours do.
- The four cameras of each scene's rig (`configs/sensors.yaml`): the uncooled LWIR camera sets the 640 x 512 grid at a
  field of view of 5-46°; the RGB, cooled-LWIR and MWIR zoom cameras cover it as far as their lenses allow.

## The questions raised before training (2026-10-15)

### Compute

Measured on laptop A (i7-6500U, 2 cores, 8 GB), 2026-10-15:
- **Rendering** M1's open-sky scenes at 128 samples: 81, 91 and 45 s by day (the MWIR sun lamp takes about 40 % of
  that), 29-33 s at night; six scenes took 5.8 min with Blender's start-up, so 600 scenes are about 9.7 h on laptop A
  alone. Laptop B is about twice as fast, so the split is 200 scenes (A) and 400 (B): about 3-3.5 h each, in
  parallel. The 2-4 min per scene quoted before was the full dataset (horizon scenes at 256 samples).
- **Training** the compact CNN (0.37 M parameters) on 16 crops of 128 px: 0.27 s per step for a single camera, 0.37 s
  for RGB + LWIR, 0.50 s for the four-camera teacher, 0.56 s for the student with the teacher running; a whole
  validation frame takes 0.13-0.24 s. With its validation, a model takes about 25 min (single camera) to 45 min (teacher,
  students) on laptop A, about half that on B: all seven are about 4 h of laptop A. The full detector needed 12-17 s per
  step of 8 whole frames (1-2 weeks for seven models); it stays for M2.
- **Camera-frame cache:** 2 s per training frame of the four cameras (registration jitter), 0.4 s per frame of the
  single-camera set, about 1.1 s per whole validation or test frame (laptop A).
- **GPU.** M1 needs none. For M2 (720+ scenes with horizons at 256 samples, the full detector on whole frames) the
  booklet's pay-as-you-go GPU rental is worth requesting: Cycles on a GPU and the detector's 12-17 s CPU step are where
  it saves days.

### Teacher camera coverage: per-pixel coverage masks

The cooled-LWIR zoom is 25° at its widest (21.7 mm on 640 x 15 µm) and the MWIR zoom 35.5-46°, while the uncooled
camera's field reaches 46°. In 86 scenes sampled with the M1 overlay the cooled camera covered only part of the frame
in 29 % and MWIR in 10 %; the manifest records the exact counts per split and in how many scenes each camera saw the
target. Instead of telling the network a band is present when part of it is empty:
- each band's **coverage mask** (1 where its camera sees the scene) enters the network as a plane at stride 4, in
  place of the band-present flag (with full coverage it equals the old flag exactly; tested);
- the band's frame is destriped and standardised **inside its covered box only** and is 0 (no contrast) outside, so
  the empty border does not bias the stripe filter or the frame's median and spread;
- crops carry their masks; a single-camera model is never taught a target its camera cannot see; an unseen target's
  contrast-to-noise is 0 in the distillation weights;
- results are also reported for the scenes where the cooled and MWIR cameras saw or missed the target.

### Crop training and false alarms

Crops are 4-7x cheaper per step than whole frames, and they do not hide false alarms, because every false-alarm number
is measured on whole frames:
- training crops are 50 % target, 25 % sky from the same frames and 25 % from target-free renders of the scene, at
  every pyramid level (1x, 2x, 4x); five times during training the model runs on 48 whole target-free frames and up to a
  third of later sky crops are its own false alarms;
- checkpoints are chosen on whole validation frames (2 present + 8 target-free per scene) by FROC, the detection
  probability at 0.005-0.05 false alarms per target-free frame;
- the operating thresholds (0.01 and 0.05 false alarms per frame) come from those frames' scores, and the test reports
  the false-alarm rate they actually give on 4 target-free whole frames per test scene, with confirmed false tracks on
  target-free video (`sequences`).

What crops do change is the score scale: targets are about 20x more frequent in crops than in whole frames. The
thresholds set on whole frames absorb that; ranking sky below targets is what the background and mined crops train.

### The M1 v2 fixes carried over

All of them: target-free background crops and false-alarm mining; LWIR destriping before the network (now for every
thermal camera, inside its coverage); 8-bit video and per-frame robust standardisation; box size and offset trained
over the target's core with a GIoU term; FROC checkpoint selection with exact false-alarm thresholds.

### Control student

`student_ctrl` is trained exactly like `student` (same batches, losses and budget) but distilled from `rgb_lwir`,
which sees only the student's own cameras. `student - student_ctrl` is therefore the gain that the cooled-LWIR and
MWIR cameras can be credited with; `student_ctrl - rgb_lwir` is distillation's own effect.

## Architecture

**M1 preliminary model** (`models/cnn_detector.py`, `CNNDetector`):
- **Size:** 0.37 M parameters (student) or 0.39 M (teacher). A compact, fully convolutional CenterNet-style detector
  for small targets.
- **Stems:** one per sensor. Each starts with a lossless 2×2 space-to-depth step, so a 2 px target keeps every pixel,
  then two convolutions take it to stride 4.
- **Fusion:** concatenation at stride 4, plus one coverage plane per sensor (where that camera sees, × whether it is on).
- **Encoder:** residual blocks at strides 4, 8 and 16; dilations 1, 2 and 4 widen the context without losing resolution.
- **Neck:** top-down, back to stride 4.
- **Heads:**
  - a target-centre heatmap (focal loss);
  - the sub-cell offset;
  - log width and height, trained over the target's core with a GIoU term.
- **Inference:** an image pyramid (1×, 2×, 4×) for large targets.
- **Training:** 128 px crops, as described below.
- **Distillation:** response distillation (softened heatmap, boxes where the teacher is confident) plus feature
  distillation at strides 4 and 8. The feature term is masked to objects and weighted by how much better the teacher
  sees each one (contrast-to-noise gap). Targets the student cannot see are left out of both terms (2026-10-18). The
  adapters are discarded after training.

**Full detector**, the proper-M1 / M2 model (`models/detector.py`, `Detector`):
- **Size:** 5.6 M parameters (student) to 5.7 M (teacher). A YOLOv8-style one-stage detector.
- **Fusion:** per-sensor stems, then mid-level fusion at strides 4 and 8 with presence flags (optional SE weighting).
- **Backbone and neck:** C2f / SPPF backbone; PAN neck with a stride-4 (P2) level for 2–8 px targets.
- **Heads:** decoupled anchor-free heads for 7 classes (target and six hard-negative classes). Boxes are distributions
  (distribution focal loss).
- **Training:** whole frames (`train/engine.py`, `models/distill.py`). It is wired and smoke-tested; on this laptop's
  CPU it takes 12–17 s per step.

## The case taxonomy: what the teacher and student should learn

Whether the four-sensor teacher adds anything over RGB + uncooled LWIR alone: the theory, the published gains,
this project's signal-regime census and the experiment that would settle it are in `docs/privileged_teacher_case.md`
(2026-10-17, corrected 2026-10-18). In M1's scenes the privileged cameras see better in 71 % of cases. But an ideal
detector already finds 95 % of targets with the student's two cameras, and the teacher's lead is at a trained
detector's working point (20–32 % of targets). What to do for better detection: `docs/detection_gain_plan.md`.

The original intent (three sensors then: RGB, LWIR, SWIR):
- the teacher should detect from any single sensor;
- from any two, where:
  - both are detectable;
  - one is detectable and the other faint, with the faint one learned so it later works alone;
  - both are faint and must be combined;
- from all three, with every mix of detectable and faint signals and with the roles swapped;
- the RGB + LWIR student should inherit all of it.

How the current design maps it, with the four sensors (SWIR deferred):

| Case | What the design does | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| One sensor alone | Teacher trains on every single-sensor subset; single-camera references | `teacher@<sensor>_only`, `lwir_only`, `lwir_cooled_only`, `mwir_only` |
| A sensor turned off later | Band dropout (students); subset dropout (teacher) | `student@lwir_only`, `student@rgb_only`, `teacher@rgb_lwir` |
| One detectable, another faint: learn the faint one | Supervised training on the faint sensor alone with the true label (synthetic data labels every sensor); distillation weighted to objects the teacher sees much better | "student faint, privileged detectable" case |
| All faint: combine | Fusion of per-sensor features before detection; the network learns to sum weak evidence | "all faint" case |
| Roles swapped | Dropout is symmetric; the regime cases count every sensor pattern | pattern counts (D/F/N per sensor) |
| Student inherits the teacher | Distillation; the control student isolates the privileged cameras' share | `student − student_ctrl` |

The regime cases use each sensor's contrast-to-noise on the target (box mean against a 2-px ring, in that sensor's
network-input frame) and only targets up to 16 px: for larger ones the box holds much sky and the plume widens the
ring, so the number understates them.

Cases the original list did not have, now part of the design or the report:
- a camera that does not cover the target (coverage masks);
- misregistered cameras (registration jitter in training);
- partly covered frames;
- cameras with very different pixel sizes (each emulated on its own pixels);
- target polarity (hotter or colder than the sky, per sensor; learned from data).

Still missing:
- a sensor that shows a false target while the others show nothing. This needs birds, aircraft and clutter, which are
  M2's hard negatives;
- temporal accumulation of faint signals over frames (single frames only);
- a saturated or sun-blinded sensor (sun in the field of view is in the data; a "blinded camera" input is not).

**Limits that no design removes.** A sensor below its noise floor (contrast-to-noise near 0) carries no information.
The student cannot detect a target that only the cooled LWIR or MWIR camera sees ("student no signal, privileged
detectable"). Distillation can only help where RGB or LWIR carry some signal, however faint, and the regime cases
measure exactly that. In real (unlabelled) data, "learn the faint sensor from the strong one" would need cross-sensor
pseudo-labels; in synthetic data every sensor has the true label.

**Weak points of the current models:**
- one training seed;
- the compact CNN's capacity;
- 360 training scenes;
- one target per frame and no clutter except clouds, which can already score as large targets (noise verification,
  `docs/sensor_noise.md`);
- single-frame decisions;
- the sensor models, not the cameras' processing: training on the camera models did not pass the verification, so the
  networks have not learned AGC'd video;
- the reporting thresholds (detectable ≥ 3, faint 1–3) are definitions, not physics.

**Proper M1 with the full detector: free GPUs.** On this CPU the full detector needs 1–2 weeks for seven models.

resumable runs, not 10-hour jobs.

Plan for Kaggle:
1. Upload the code bundle (`export-code`, ~35 MB) and the rendered scenes (~3 GB) as private datasets.
2. In a CPU session (not counted against the GPU quota), build the frame cache into its saved output.
3. In GPU sessions, train from that cache. A step that takes 12–17 s here should take well under a second on a T4,
   so seven models need a few GPU-hours.
4. Download the runs and import them (`import-results`).

To do before this works:
- device and mixed-precision support and a cache reader in the full-detector trainer (about half a day);
- a Kaggle notebook;
- optionally, Blender rendering on the GPU, which would cut the 720-scene full dataset from 30–45 CPU-hours to a few
  GPU-hours (untested).

The booklet's paid GPU rental is the fallback if the free quota is not enough.

## The M1 v2 diagnosis these fixes come from (2026-10-04)

The validation diagnosis of the earlier compact-CNN pilot (validation scenes only; no test scene was read):

| Problem seen | Cause found | v2 fix |
|---|---|---|
| False alarms on empty sky (0.045 per frame at the usable threshold) | 4 of 6 were 134-212 px boxes: the 2x/4x pyramid levels had only trained on scenes with large targets | Background and target-free crops at every pyramid level; mined false alarms are fed back as training crops |
| The rest sat on LWIR column stripes | No stripe handling; the simulated stripes were also unrealistically constant | Realistic stripe model (`noise/lwir_fpn.py`); stripe filter before the network (`models/cnn_detector.destripe`) |
| 6 of 8 missed targets were 2-4 px with scores 0.16-0.32 | The threshold had to sit above the structured false alarms | Removing those lets the operating threshold fall |
| Boxes in the right place but the wrong size | Box size trained at one grid cell only | Size and offset trained over the target's Gaussian core, plus a GIoU loss |
| Thresholds at the grid ceiling; selection on 33 frames | Too little validation data; coarse grid | ~58 validation scenes x (2 present + 8 target-free frames); exact false-alarm thresholds |
| Teacher did not beat the baseline (SWIR) | SWIR carried no unique information there | SWIR deferred; the teacher now sees the cooled-LWIR and MWIR cameras, each rendered in its own band |

**Realistic stripes.** Uncooled cameras keep row and column noise after shutter correction, and it grows between
corrections (FLIR, US 9,235,876). On top of each rig camera's NETD and row/column temporal noise, the uncooled camera
gets, as [C] assumptions (`tools/m1_det2.py` `LWIR_REALISM`): a column gain error of 0.1-0.5 % (the stripe changes
wherever the scene changes down the column) and column drift along the readout up to 0.05 K. The cooled cameras keep
their own residual non-uniformity from the rig (< 0.04 % of the dynamic range). The filter estimates stripes in
overlapping 256-row bands and also removes row noise; on a synthetic test it cut the error from 0.088 to 0.024 against
a 0.016 noise floor (`tests/test_det2.py`), so the network is trained on the residual stripes, not on clean frames.

## Limits

- One training seed and a compact 0.37 M-parameter CNN, not the full detector.
- Synthetic open sky only: no birds, aircraft, terrain or other clutter; clouds as the station hour reports them.
- Generic camera classes and assumed stripe parameters; no real-camera data, so the sim-to-real gap is untested.
- 180 test scenes: intervals are wide for single size bins and for the coverage slices.

A null or negative distillation result is reported as such.
